Notices arrive late

On Sunday, September 13, someone with measles spent the evening in Terminals B and F at Philadelphia International Airport. The notice went up on September 19.

By then the 72-hour window for the vaccine had closed. For babies and pregnant travellers, a few hours remained for immune globulin. That is not one slow department. It is how notices work: someone gets sick, a test comes back, the health department traces where they went, then it publishes.

Scattered notices, one clock

Every health department posts its own notices: a press release here, a list page there. Seventy-Two reads them all and keeps the one that matters to you running as a clock.

  1. Read

    Every 30 minutes, AWS Step Functions fetches every health department page we watch. Claude reads each new notice and copies out the places and hours, each with the exact words it came from. Code checks every one before it is kept.

  2. Match

    You say where you were in your own words. Claude turns that into places and times. Code then decides whether your hours overlap a notice's hours and works out your deadlines from the CDC's rules.

  3. Remind

    Start your clock and a workflow of your own waits for each deadline, reminds you before it, and stops when you tell it you acted. Add it to your calendar and it reminds you even if you never come back.

Not a memory anymore

This week the United States lost its measles elimination status, after 26 years.

2026 has had more than 5,000 confirmed cases in 45 states, the most since 1991. Measles spreads through the air and lingers for up to two hours after an infected person leaves. That is why airports, emergency rooms and stores keep turning up in notices.

Questions

Short answers. Every one points to its source.

MedicalIs this medical advice?

No. It shows what health departments have published and the CDC's timing rules. Your doctor or health department decides what you should do. When in doubt, call them, and call before you go in so they can protect others.

VaccinatedI had MMR as a child. Do I need to do anything?

Written proof of two MMR doses, a blood test showing immunity, or being born before 1957 usually means you are protected. You do not need the vaccine after an exposure. Still watch for fever and rash for 21 days.

Missed itMy 72 hours have passed. Now what?

Immune globulin can still help for up to 6 days, mainly for babies, pregnant people and people with weak immune systems. After that, watch for symptoms until day 21 and call before going in if any start.

DataWhere do the notices come from?

Only from health departments' own published pages. Every window links to the original notice and quotes the exact words it came from. If a notice does not give clock times, we say so and never guess.

PrivacyWhat do you keep about me?

Only what you type, and only while it can still matter. Clocks delete themselves the day after your watch ends. Things you paste into the Lab delete themselves within a day. No accounts, no tracking, no location.

CoverageDo you cover every health department?

Not yet. We read 27 pages from 12 health departments in 9 states, and the list is growing. If your notice is not here, paste it into the Lab.

Nothing to look after

Seventy-Two is built entirely from serverless AWS services. No servers, no deployment pipeline. It reads the news on a schedule, and each person's clock is its own Step Functions workflow that costs nothing while it waits.

How it is built, step by step
Every service, model, library and tool

  • EventBridge Scheduler starts a read every 30 minutes
  • Step Functions reads every source in parallel, and runs one clock per person
  • Lambda fetches pages, reads them and answers checks
  • Bedrock runs Claude Haiku, which reads notices and trips
  • DynamoDB holds windows, saved trips and clocks, and deletes them on schedule
  • DynamoDB Streams sends each new window to every saved trip, the moment it is stored
  • S3 keeps every notice we read, and a backup of the board
  • API Gateway and CloudFront serve the site and fail over to S3
